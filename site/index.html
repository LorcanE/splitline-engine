The problem

A percentile is not a judgement. It is the start of one.

Every February you have the same conversation eight times. An athlete shows you a number, you say something encouraging, and neither of you knows whether the number was good. The leaderboard sorts 233,072 people into one column and stops there.

What you actually need is three things it will not give you: how they did against people their own age, which of the three workouts is dragging their ranking down, and how many places fixing it is worth. All three are computable from the public field. Nobody computes them, because it takes a full crawl of 4,663 leaderboard pages to build the tables.

That crawl is what you are buying.

What it computes

Four columns you cannot get anywhere else

Percentile

Place against finishers only — athletes with a valid score on every workout. Counting the people who quit flatters everyone by about fifteen points.

1,200 of 105,959 → 1.1%

Age-adjusted

How far ahead of the median athlete in their own five-year age band they finished. This is the number worth showing a 44-year-old.

+55.4% vs the 40–44 median

Weakest workout

Named, not guessed. The workout where their placing is furthest behind their own standard — which is where their ranking is being set.

WK3

Places to gain

What closing that one gap is worth, in places. It turns a training argument into arithmetic, and it is nearly always the cheapest improvement available.

Why you can trust the numbers

The limits are printed inside the file

Everything comes from the public CrossFit Games Open leaderboard, crawled in full. No estimates, no modelled fill-ins, no proprietary index that cannot be checked.

Height and weight are self-reported and often left blank, so anything involving body composition covers a subset of the field and says so. Age bands need at least thirty finishers before they appear at all. Cutline scores are the neighbourhood of a threshold, not an exact boundary, because scores are discrete and percentiles are not.

If you find a figure you think is wrong, send it over. Corrections get published, not quietly patched.

Questions

Before you buy

Do I need my athletes' full leaderboard data?

No. You need four things per athlete: division, age, overall place, and their place on each workout. All four are on their public leaderboard profile.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. The lookups deliberately avoid array formulas, so they behave the same in Excel, Numbers, Google Sheets and LibreOffice. No macros to enable and nothing to install.

Why not just use the leaderboard filters?

The leaderboard filters to a division. It will not tell you the median percentile of a 44-year-old, because that requires the whole field at once. That is the table this file contains.
