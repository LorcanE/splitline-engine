The problem

Nobody paces a HYROX by dividing by eight

And yet that is what most race plans amount to. A HYROX is not eight equal efforts — it is eight runs, eight stations and a transition zone that each behave differently, and the shape of the race changes with the time you are chasing.

A 65-minute athlete and a 95-minute athlete do not run the same race slower and faster. They run different races. The wall balls take one of them under five minutes and the other nearly fifteen. Pacing off a percentage of your goal time gets both of them wrong.

Why you can trust the numbers

The limits are printed inside the file

Every figure comes from the published per-athlete split tables at results.hyrox.com. An athlete is used only if their sixteen splits plus roxzone reconstruct their published finish time to within 2% — 36 of 880 failed that test and were dropped rather than quietly averaged in.

This edition is built from one race. Course layout, floor surface and conditions all move splits a little, so treat the plan as a well-founded starting point rather than a law. It is rebuilt as more races are added, and buyers get every rebuild.

If you find a figure you think is wrong, send it over. Corrections get published, not quietly patched.

Questions

Before you buy

What do I need to enter?

One number: your target finish time in minutes. And whether you are racing the men's or women's division.

Why do men and women get different plans?

Because at the same finish time they run measurably different races. Women in this data are almost a minute faster on the sandbag lunges and forty seconds slower on the ski erg. Pacing a woman off a man's plan puts her behind exactly where she can least afford it.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. The lookups deliberately avoid array formulas, so the plan behaves identically in Excel, Numbers, Google Sheets and LibreOffice. No macros, nothing to install.

Is this an elite plan or a first-timer plan?

Both. Any whole target between 60 and 140 minutes. The race shape changes across that range, which is the whole point.
