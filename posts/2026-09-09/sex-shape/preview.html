Same finish time, completely different race

82 men and 81 women who finished between 85 and 95 minutes. Matching on finish time strips out speed and leaves only shape.

09 September 2026 · Splitline

Comparing men's and women's HYROX times tells you nothing except that men are faster. The interesting question is what happens when you hold finish time fixed — take only the athletes who finished within the same ten-minute window, and ask where each group spent it.

Each bar is the women's median minus the men's median for that segment. Bars to the left are segments where women were faster.

The biggest gap in the women's favour is 100m Sandbag Lunges, where they were 0:58 quicker. The biggest gap the other way is All eight runs, where they were 1:18 slower. Between those two extremes there is 2:16 of spread — inside a race where, by construction, both groups finished within ten minutes of each other.

What it means if you are coaching

The pattern is consistent rather than random: at matched finish time the ergs and the runs cost women time, and the strength and skill stations give it back. Pacing a woman off a man's splits therefore puts her behind on exactly the segments where she has least room to recover, and hands her a cushion where she needed none.

If you hand every athlete in your gym the same race plan, half of them are being paced off the wrong distribution.
