The slowest athletes do not run slower — they collapse

Run 1 against run 8 for 844 finishers, grouped by finish time.

13 September 2026 · Splitline

A HYROX is eight one-kilometre runs with a station between each. The interesting number is not how fast the first one is — it is how much slower the last one is than the first.

Higher means the athlete faded more over the race.

Athletes finishing in the <70 band ran their last kilometre 1.6% slower than their first — 4:05 out to 4:09. Athletes in the 120+ band went from 6:18 to 9:36, a fade of 52.4%.

This is a pacing problem, not a fitness problem

A fade that size is not what running out of fitness looks like. It is what starting too fast looks like. The first kilometre of a HYROX is run in a crowd, on fresh legs, with a clock that has just started — and it is the single easiest place in the sport to lose ten minutes you will never get back.

If your run 8 is more than about 10% slower than your run 1, your race was decided in the first five minutes.
